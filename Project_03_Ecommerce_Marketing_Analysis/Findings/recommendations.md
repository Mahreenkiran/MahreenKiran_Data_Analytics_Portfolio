# Business Recommendations

## Overview

The analysis identified several opportunities to improve conversion, revenue growth, customer retention and operational performance.

The recommendations below are based on the strongest patterns observed across website traffic, marketing channels, customer behaviour, order performance and refunds.

---

## 1. Prioritise Mobile Conversion Optimisation

Mobile users converted at approximately **3.09%**, compared with **8.50% for desktop users**.

This represents one of the clearest performance gaps in the analysis.

Recommended actions:

- review the mobile checkout and navigation experience
- test page speed and usability on smaller screens
- simplify form completion and purchase steps
- investigate whether key product or checkout content is harder to access on mobile
- compare mobile drop-off rates across funnel stages

Improving the mobile journey could increase conversion without requiring additional traffic acquisition.

---

## 2. Improve Product-to-Cart Progression

The largest customer journey drop occurred between **Product Views and Cart**.

Of approximately 261,231 sessions reaching the products stage, only around 94,953 progressed to the cart.

This means that approximately **63.7% of product-view sessions did not continue to cart**.

Recommended actions:

- strengthen product page calls to action
- improve product descriptions and value communication
- make pricing and purchase options more prominent
- test different product page layouts
- reduce friction between product viewing and cart entry
- investigate whether specific products have lower product-to-cart progression

This stage should be a priority because even a relatively small improvement could materially increase completed orders.

---

## 3. Continue Investing in High-Performing Paid Search

Paid Search generated approximately **$1.48M in net revenue**, making it the strongest marketing channel by commercial contribution.

Recommended actions:

- protect budget allocation to high-performing Paid Search campaigns
- identify the keywords and campaigns associated with the strongest conversion rates
- monitor customer acquisition efficiency alongside revenue
- test whether successful Paid Search messaging can be applied to weaker channels

Paid Search should remain a core acquisition channel while performance is monitored for efficiency and diminishing returns.

---

## 4. Review Paid Social Performance

Paid Social converted at approximately **3.21%**, considerably below the strongest channels.

Recommended actions:

- review audience targeting and campaign objectives
- compare landing pages used by Paid Social traffic with stronger-performing sources
- test different creative, messaging and offers
- assess whether Paid Social is serving awareness rather than direct conversion objectives
- avoid increasing spend until conversion performance is better understood

The channel should not necessarily be removed, but its role in the acquisition strategy should be reviewed.

---

## 5. Apply Learnings from the Best-Converting Landing Pages

`/lander-5` achieved the highest observed landing-page conversion rate at approximately **10.17%**.

Recommended actions:

- compare its layout, messaging and call-to-action structure with lower-performing landing pages
- identify design elements that may be contributing to stronger conversion
- test similar page structures on high-traffic landing pages
- use controlled A/B testing before rolling out major changes

The objective should be to transfer successful characteristics from the strongest landing pages to pages receiving greater traffic.

---

## 6. Encourage Repeat Visits and Customer Retention

Repeat users converted at approximately **7.40%**, compared with **6.62% for non-repeat users**.

Later customer sessions also converted more strongly than first sessions.

Recommended actions:

- encourage users to return through remarketing or email campaigns
- create targeted offers for returning visitors
- personalise messaging for repeat customers where possible
- analyse the typical time between visits to inform re-engagement timing
- monitor conversion by session sequence over time

Returning customers appear to have higher purchase intent, making retention activity commercially valuable.

---

## 7. Increase Multi-Item Purchases

Multi-item orders represented approximately **23.87% of orders**, but produced substantially higher value.

Average revenue increased from approximately:

- **$50.82 for single-item orders**
- to **$89.25 for multi-item orders**

Average gross profit also increased materially.

Recommended actions:

- introduce cross-sell recommendations
- promote complementary products on product and cart pages
- test product bundles
- consider order-value thresholds for promotional incentives
- highlight related products during checkout

Increasing the proportion of multi-item orders could improve both revenue and gross profit without requiring additional customer acquisition.

---

## 8. Monitor Product Concentration Risk

**The Original Mr. Fuzzy** generated approximately **62.5% of total item revenue**.

This indicates that a large proportion of product revenue depended on a single product.

Recommended actions:

- continue supporting the strongest-performing product
- monitor whether revenue concentration increases further
- develop demand for secondary products
- test bundles involving lower-volume products
- use cross-selling to diversify product revenue

A broader product revenue mix could reduce reliance on one dominant product.

---

## 9. Investigate Product-Level Refund Patterns

Overall refund performance remained manageable, but refund rates differed by product.

The **Birthday Sugar Panda** recorded the highest observed product refund rate at approximately **6.04%**.

Recommended actions:

- monitor refund rate by product over time
- investigate reasons for higher-refund products where data is available
- review product descriptions to ensure customer expectations are accurate
- compare refund patterns with product launches, order volume and marketing campaigns
- prioritise high-volume products when assessing the financial impact of refunds

Refund analysis should focus not only on total refund value but also on the products driving refund behaviour.

---

## 10. Track Revenue and Conversion Separately

The analysis showed that the strongest revenue month and strongest conversion month were not the same.

- **December 2014** generated the highest net revenue
- **February 2015** recorded the highest conversion rate

Recommended actions:

- monitor traffic, conversion, order volume and revenue together
- avoid using revenue alone as a measure of website efficiency
- investigate whether growth is being driven by higher traffic, better conversion or larger order values
- use month-on-month and rolling trends to distinguish short-term fluctuations from sustained performance changes

This provides a more complete view of commercial performance.

---

## Priority Actions

Based on potential business impact, the recommended priorities are:

1. **Improve mobile conversion**
2. **Reduce Product View → Cart drop-off**
3. **Protect and optimise Paid Search performance**
4. **Increase multi-item purchases**
5. **Strengthen repeat-customer engagement**
6. **Monitor product-level refund behaviour**

These actions focus on improving performance within existing traffic before relying primarily on further customer acquisition.
